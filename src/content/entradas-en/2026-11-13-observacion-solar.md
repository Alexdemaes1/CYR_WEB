## Introduction

On Friday 13 November, at the Turia campus, the Club de Ciencia y Redes held a solar observation activity. The idea came from the chance to make use of the telescopes the University has, and it was coordinated by Professor Joan Catalá. The activity aimed to bring students closer to observational research in a practical way, giving them the chance to handle real scientific equipment in a familiar, accessible setting.

The session also served as a pilot: it was used to check the condition and performance of the instruments and to lay the groundwork for future club projects.

## Goals

The main goal was to calibrate the telescopes and become familiar with how they work: assembly, alignment, focusing and use of the solar filters needed to observe the Sun safely. This groundwork is useful in two ways:

1. **Preparing the astronomy outing** the club has planned, so that participants arrive with the equipment checked and with experience in handling it.
2. **Enabling more continuous exploration from the university itself**, whether through further solar observations or by tracking the Moon through its phases.

As a complementary goal, the activity set out to encourage scientific curiosity among students. Working directly with research equipment is a great incentive to spark interest in astronomy and in the scientific method.

## Conclusions and next steps

The observation fulfilled its purpose as a test of the telescopes and made it possible to identify sunspots. Building on this experience, the club is considering new projects, such as regular tracking of the Moon or continued solar observation, as well as the upcoming astronomy outing.
